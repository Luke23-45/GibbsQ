
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Dispatching tasks to heterogeneous parallel servers is a core problem in operations research, with applications in server farms, cloud platforms, and communication networks.
The classical Join the Shortest Queue (JSQ) policy is optimal under homogeneous servers but ignores service rates.
Its extension JSSQ accounts for server speed but remains a deterministic rule without an entropy-regularized formulation equipped with formal stability bounds.
Reinforcement-learning approaches offer greater flexibility, but without guided initialization they can behave poorly in queueing systems and typically come with no stability guarantees.
We address this gap by designing routing laws that are state-aware, capacity-aware, provably stable, and compatible with neural policy architectures. Our analysis is carried out under standard Markovian assumptions (Poisson arrivals and exponential service); robustness to non-Markovian misspecification is left for future work. All logarithms in this manuscript are natural unless noted otherwise.

\subsection{Entropy-Regularized Routing}
We adopt a Gibbs-measure viewpoint: each arrival is routed by a softmax distribution over server scores, with concentration parameter $\temp > 0$ controlling the trade-off between exploratory and greedy routing.
For raw softmax, the regularizer is Shannon entropy; for UAS, it is KL divergence relative to a capacity-weighted prior.
In both cases, the log-sum-exp normalizer is the convex conjugate of the regularizer~\cite{boyd2004convex}. This variational structure yields explicit Lyapunov drift bounds while preserving the smooth outputs needed for neural policy gradients.
Entropy-regularized control is well established in reinforcement learning, including maximum-entropy methods such as Soft Actor-Critic~\cite{haarnoja2018soft}. Our contribution is to use the same variational machinery to derive explicit Foster--Lyapunov stability proofs for heterogeneous queue routing.

\subsection{Contributions}
GibbsQ yields three main findings:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Stability proofs:}
We prove positive Harris recurrence~\cite{meyn2009markov} for two routing laws under the strict load condition $\totcap = \sum_i \srvrate_i > \arrate$.
Raw softmax (Theorem~\ref{thm:softmax}) and Unified Capacity-Aware Softmax (UAS, Theorem~\ref{thm:uas}) are established via self-contained Foster--Lyapunov arguments with explicit drift constants.
We further identify a theorem-backed certified subset within the reflected family and prove positive Harris recurrence for that subset under an explicit parameter-conditional drift condition.
The UAS drift constants are independent of $\temp$.
Under the anchor benchmark, no competitive routing configuration satisfies that sufficient condition; the certified and performant regions are disjoint (Section~\ref{sec:sruas_audit}).

\item \textbf{Calibrated closed-form policy:}
Base UAS ($\temp{=}10$ at $11.50$) underperforms JSSQ ($11.02$), indicating that the provably stable parameterization sacrifices empirical performance for analytical tractability.
Reflected UAS, a three-parameter extension, achieves $\E[\sum_i \Qlen{i}] = 10.04$ under the standard benchmark, reducing steady-state queue length by $0.97$ units (8.84\%) relative to JSSQ.
The benchmark-default parameters are not certified by Theorem~\ref{thm:sruas} (post-validation audit yields $\varepsilon < 0$); a numerically verified piecewise Lyapunov candidate provides evidence for a future certification route.

\item \textbf{Teacher-choice effect in learned policies:}
The default neural policy ($\ngibbsq$), trained from UAS via behavior cloning and \reinforce{}, underperforms JSSQ ($11.68$ vs.\ $11.02$), consistent with a teacher-quality bottleneck because UAS itself underperforms JSSQ.
Switching the BC teacher to Reflected UAS substantially improves this bottleneck, yielding the lowest point estimate among all evaluated policies ($9.82$ under Protocol B, best-of-5 seed selection; matches Reflected UAS at $\approx 10.04$ under Protocol A).
This directional advantage persists across 16 generalization configurations and near-critical loads up to $\rho = 0.98$.
Training from scratch performs extremely poorly ($5{,}451$), reinforcing that initialization quality is central among the tested training design choices.
\end{enumerate}

The main practical story is therefore layered: UAS is the principal certified policy, Reflected UAS is the stronger empirical closed-form extension, and the certified subset marks the portion of that extension currently covered by theory.
