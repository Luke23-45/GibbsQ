
\begin{abstract}
Routing policies for heterogeneous parallel queues face a fundamental tension between state-awareness and capacity-awareness. While classical policies typically prioritize only one, and standard reinforcement learning (RL) policy-gradient approaches without guided initialization typically lack formal stability, we propose GibbsQ---a framework that bridges this gap via entropy-regularized routing.

At the \textbf{analytical level}, we formally prove positive Harris recurrence for two routing laws (raw softmax and UAS) under the strict load condition $\totcap > \arrate$. We further establish a parameter-conditional stability certificate for a calibrated subclass (SCUAS); however, the sufficient condition is not satisfied by the benchmark-default parameters, leaving the strongest empirical configurations uncertified at present. These proofs yield computable drift constants; notably, the UAS constants are temperature-independent.

At the \textbf{empirical level}, we introduce Calibrated UAS, a closed-form extension of UAS with three tunable parameters. We find that while Calibrated UAS delivers superior performance---reducing steady-state queue lengths by 8.84\% and 12.6\% relative to JSSQ and baseline UAS respectively---the benchmark-default setting is not theorem-certified. A post-validation audit confirms that the audited benchmark configuration yields negative sufficient drift constants, thus these results remain empirical rather than certified in this study. However, we identify a benchmark-specific piecewise Lyapunov candidate that materially narrows the case for future certification.

Finally, we quantify the \textbf{teacher-choice effect} in the learned-policy layer. The default neural policy (trained from the analytical UAS teacher) underperforms both JSSQ and UAS. However, under a controlled extended-horizon protocol (Protocol B), a neural variant initialized from Calibrated UAS via behavior cloning, fine-tuned with \reinforce{}, and selected as the best of 5 random seeds on a held-out validation set, achieves a mean queue of $9.821$---the lowest point estimate among all policies under Protocol B, outperforming its closed-form teacher ($9.898$) by $\Delta = -0.077 \pm 0.025$ (95\% CI, paired). Under the primary Protocol A, this variant matches Calibrated UAS at $\approx 10.04$. The best-of-5 selection introduces optimistic bias; without preserved per-seed logs, the unbiased ensemble mean cannot be computed. This directional advantage persists across 16 generalization configurations and into near-critical loads up to $\rho = 0.98$ (8.8\% improvement with uncertainty underestimated at near-critical loads; see Table~\ref{tab:critical}). Our formal stability proofs and empirical evaluations assume standard M/M/1 dynamics; robustness to model misspecification remains outside the current scope.
\end{abstract}
