\label{sec:appendix_training}

\subsection{Phase~1: Behavior Cloning and Phase~2: REINFORCE}

The Neural GibbsQ pipeline depends on two sequential phases. The preliminary BC phase initializes the neural policy by supervised imitation of an analytical teacher~\cite{ross2011reduction}. The network is uniquely trained to minimize the cross-entropy between its modeled softmax output and the teacher's action distribution. At convergence, the network broadly reproduces the teacher's routing distribution, providing a distributionally stable initialization. Starting from the BC-pretrained weights, we apply the \reinforce{} policy gradient algorithm~\cite{williams1992simple} to directly optimize the steady-state mean total queue length over an ensemble of 5 seeds to smooth short-horizon trajectory randomness. Table~\ref{tab:unified_training_config} provides the consolidated configuration and ablation parameters.

The exact same network architecture, preprocessing, learning rate, and optimizer settings are applied to the calibrated-teacher ablation variant, yielding comparable target fidelity. For \reinforce{}, no gradient clipping or entropy regularization bonus is applied during training; the BC initialization provides sufficient stability to avoid catastrophic divergence. Convergence is typically observed by epoch 6--8 under the ablation budget, verifying 8 epochs as sufficient for this architecture. The 32.6\% magnitude error relative to finite-difference gradients (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_gradient}) effectively reduces the learning rate but does not affect the optimization direction.

The key finding is that teacher choice ($1.32$-unit improvement) dominates all architectural modifications ($< 0.2$-unit effects), while training from scratch without BC diverges catastrophically (mean queue $> 5{,}000$).

\paragraph{Training--evaluation horizon mismatch}
The ablation training episodes use a horizon of 750 time units, while the evaluation protocol (Protocol~B) uses 20{,}000 time units. This 26$\times$ disparity may introduce a distributional mismatch between the training and evaluation regimes.

\paragraph{Compute budget}
Wall-clock training times were not systematically logged; the total compute budget for the full experimental campaign is approximately 200 CPU-hours.

\section{Piecewise Lyapunov Candidate Search}
\label{sec:appendix_piecewise_search}

The mathematically formulated piecewise Lyapunov candidate presented in Section~\ref{sec:caluas_piecewise_candidate} (Equation 67) was numerically discovered using an automated post-validation search procedure. The configuration evaluates a heavily parameterized convex combination scalarizing various structural components bounding the state space:
1. Base heterogeneous quadratic bound ($\frac{1}{2} \sum_i \Qlen{i}^2 / \mu_i$)
2. Unweighted state limits $(\sum_i \Qlen{i})^2$
3. Smoothed polynomial queue bounds ($\sum_i \Qlen{i}^3 / \mu_i$)
4. Maximum-queue polynomial bounds ($\max_i \Qlen{i}^2$)

The scalar numerical coefficients were jointly optimized using L-BFGS-B minimization to strictly minimize the supremum of the CTMC generator $\gen \lyap_{\mathrm{PW}}(\Qstate)$ evaluated exactly across the state space shells structurally bounding normal-load stationarity ($|\Qstate|_1 \in \{12, \dots, 20\}$). Counter-example tail limit states that repeatedly violated negative boundary drift bounds during concurrent random-check loop verifications were dynamically prioritized in the search weight evaluation matrix. The final convergence weights determining the precise mixture were extracted and locked to 6 decimal precision points explicitly for robust empirical capsule testing.
